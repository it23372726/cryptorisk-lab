How to use CryptoRisk Lab

  1. Choose your scenario. Set the horizon, paths per model and investment. Use the same settings and seed to reproduce a run.
  2. Start the simulation. Pause and resume to inspect progress. Reset clears results and keeps your settings.
  3. Compare outcomes. Switch the price chart between models at any time. Read the terminal loss probabilities and tail losses in the risk table.
Models and calibration

GBM uses a constant volatility and independent normal daily log returns. The Markov model generates each daily return in the current calm or volatile state, then samples the next state.

Parameters were fitted to Binance BTC/USDT daily observations from 2022–2024. Held-out evaluation in 2025 showed modest descriptive improvement for Markov. Its superiority was statistically inconclusive after multiple-comparison adjustment.

Verification and interpretation

The simulator checks output structure, analytical GBM probabilities and the ordering of VaR and Expected Shortfall. For 30 days at 1.00× volatility it also compares Markov probabilities with notebook references. Probability checks allow four standard errors for Monte Carlo variation.

These checks assess numerical agreement. Real markets can experience shocks outside either model. This simulator is intended for education and research, not personalised financial advice.

The Markov forecasts above average over the actual initial regimes observed in 2025 (269 Calm and 66 Volatile). Conditional estimates range from 19.58% to 21.93% for ≥10% losses, and from 5.16% to 6.64% for ≥20% losses. All probabilities refer to 30-day outcomes.
